Halloween III: Season of the Witch was released in 1982.
Tommy Lee Wallace directed Halloween III: Season of the Witch.
Tommy Lee Wallace also wrote the film's screenplay.
John Carpenter and Debra Hill served as producers.
Unlike the first two Halloween films, this story does not center on Michael Myers.
The film follows Dr. Daniel Challis, played by Tom Atkins.
Stacey Nelkin plays Ellie Grimbridge.
Dan O'Herlihy plays the mysterious toy-company owner Conal Cochran.
The fictional company behind the masks is called Silver Shamrock Novelties.
The three featured mask designs are a skull, a witch, and a jack-o'-lantern.
A catchy Silver Shamrock television jingle is a recurring part of the story.
The plot revolves around a sinister plan tied to Halloween masks and a television commercial.
Stonehenge plays an important role in Conal Cochran's plan.
The story is set around the fictional Northern California town of Santa Mira.
The film was distributed by Universal Pictures.
John Carpenter and Alan Howarth composed the film's electronic score.
The soundtrack uses synthesizers to create much of its eerie atmosphere.
Dean Cundey was the cinematographer for Halloween III: Season of the Witch.
The movie runs for approximately 98 minutes.
The film's original theatrical release date in the United States was October 22, 1982.
The movie was part of an attempt to explore Halloween stories beyond Michael Myers.
The title's “Season of the Witch” phrase gives the story a folk-horror feel.
A central part of the scheme is a broadcast that is timed for Halloween night.
The film's tagline is “The night no one comes home!”
Halloween III became a cult favorite among many horror fans after its initial release.
The opening follows a frightened man carrying a Halloween mask while he is pursued.
The film mixes science-fiction elements with its Halloween-themed horror.
The Silver Shamrock masks are presented as a hugely popular product for children.
The story's climax depends on the television broadcast reaching children wearing the masks.
The movie is often discussed as the most unusual entry in the original Halloween film series because of its standalone story.
